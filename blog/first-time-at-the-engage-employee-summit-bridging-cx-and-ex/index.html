First Time at the Engage Employee Summit: Bridging CX and EX

May 20264 min readBy Jaime Valle
Blog#employee experience#EX#customer experience#CX#Engage Employee Summit#CX and EX#survey response rates#representation#trust#closed-loop feedback#you said we did#Kaizen#continuous improvement#employee listening#employee engagement#VoE#voice of the employee#event recap#HR#people experience

Introduction

It was my first time attending the Engage Employee Summit, and what a remarkable day it turned out to be. A huge thank you to Teresa Monroe for the kind invitation, which gave me the perfect opportunity to spend a full day learning alongside my colleague and great CX advisor, Stéphanie Gardon.

For a long time, my focus has been firmly on the CX side of things. I have been studying the science behind it, the culture, the KPIs, the authors, the models… And then, as a CX consultant, using that knowledge to help businesses truly listen to and understand their customers. Lately, though, I’ve been paying much closer attention to the EX world, where the spotlight is on employees. And I have to say, the more I dig into that area, the more I enjoy it.

Of course, CX and EX are deeply connected, yet at the same time, completely different. EX feels like a bespoke version of CX, tailored specifically for the real people—the colleagues around us who we speak to every day, and who sit between the business and the final customer.

Inspirational keynotes and fresh perspectives

It was genuinely interesting (and a lot of fun!) to sit through several of the keynotes. The speakers were inspirational and packed their sessions with valuable insights and plenty of food for thought. I particularly enjoyed Nick Court’s presentation, along with those from Francesca Woodhouse, Kaylee Darkins, Kelly Newman, Charlotte Grant, and many others.

The topics felt refreshingly current and relevant. I kept hearing familiar concepts that I’ve spoken about, written about, and lived by for years, but now reframed within the EX universe. It was incredibly insightful and made me see old ideas in a new light.

One recurring theme that had me smiling (and occasionally wanting to cheer) was the issue of low response rates. Every time it came up, I had to stop myself from shouting, “Don’t worry about the response rate—focus on representation!” If you’ve read my previous posts on chasing response rates, you’ll know exactly where I stand. Stop losing sleep over a 5% response rate; it could still give you a massively representative view if done right.

Another topic that kept surfacing was trust. It’s fundamental in CX, and naturally just as critical for employees. At its heart, it’s about meeting expectations and doing exactly what you said you would do. It sounds simple, but for many businesses and employers, it’s something that gets ticked in a strategy document rather than lived out in practice.

The graph that always makes me smile

In one of the sessions, Ian Wallis from HMRC shared an image on screen that made me grin instantly. I couldn’t resist snapping a picture. I’ve seen this graph (or very close versions of it) countless times, and yes, I’ve used one myself!

The message is crystal clear: you’re not finished when the survey closes. In fact, that’s when the real work begins. The exciting part of the cycle is acting on what you’ve learned and turning those insights into genuine improvements.

As Ian wisely pointed out, and as some of us have been championing for years, communicating what you’ve done is just as vital. The classic “you said, we did” update closes the loop beautifully. I loved his marketing-inspired take on it: “Tell them, remind them, and then tell them that you told them.” It’s not just about sharing results; it’s about communicating clearly and effectively so everyone knows what followed.

What I took away

All in all, the Engage Employee Summit left me energised and even more convinced that CX and EX thrive when treated as two sides of the same coin. I came away with fresh ideas, renewed motivation, and a stronger admiration for the Employee Experience space.

If you’re exploring how to better connect customer and employee insights in your own organisation, I’d love to hear your thoughts. Drop me a line or connect on LinkedIn. Let’s keep the conversation going!

Here’s to listening better, acting smarter, and building trust that actually lasts.
